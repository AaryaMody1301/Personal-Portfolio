02 / The problem

Start with the right question.

A dashboard can display a change without explaining the reporting period, source concept, or underlying filing. Research becomes harder to repeat when facts, calculations, and interpretation lose those connections.

03 / My contribution

What I built.

I built the application around SEC identity resolution and ingestion, a PostgreSQL/Prisma evidence model, deterministic change detection, and an evidence-linked research workflow. Optional AI consumes bounded grounding packets instead of replacing the source record.

04 / The approach

Make each step inspectable.

  1. 01

    Ingest with provenance

    Resolve ticker identity, read submissions and companyfacts, follow supported continuation history, and store filings with accession-level provenance.

  2. 02

    Normalize and compare

    Map a deliberately bounded set of metrics, retain missing values, and compare compatible reporting periods using deterministic calculations.

  3. 03

    Review the evidence

    Expose source links and stable evidence identifiers. Track research checkpoints and thesis relationships; optional AI claims must cite known grounding evidence.

05 / Evidence

What supports the story.

  • Ingestion records success, partial, or failed outcomes and uses bounded retry behavior.
  • Repeated ingestion is idempotent; missing source concepts remain missing.
  • The reviewer demo exercises the real application and PostgreSQL evidence storage with explicitly synthetic fixtures, without market-data or AI keys.

06 / Scope & limitations

Keep the boundaries visible.

The credential-free demo uses a fictional company, synthetic market data, and filing-like fixtures with example.com links. These are not genuine SEC filings or proof of a working live integration. AI is optional and disabled without configuration. The project is a research tool, not an automated stock recommendation system.

Keep source facts, deterministic calculations, and interpretation distinguishable.
